# Modeling assumptions

Every modeling assumption goes here, with the phase it affects and how it could be relaxed.

| # | Assumption | Phase | Why / how to relax |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Sound speed constants are for dry air (c = sqrt(gamma R T), gamma = 1.4). A humidity correction is added in Phase 3. | 2, 3 | Gives about 343 m/s at 20 °C, which is enough for the uniform-atmosphere phases. |
| A2 | The whole channel fires at t = 0 for each stroke, because the return stroke moves at a large fraction of light speed. | 1, 2 | The error is microseconds against acoustic times of seconds. |
| A3 | Atmosphere is horizontally stratified: profiles depend only on height. | 3 | This is standard for ray tables. Turbulence (a stretch goal) adds the 3D variation. |
| A4 | The channel is built from straight segments about 10 m long. Tortuosity is a random walk whose turn angles are independent and identically distributed: half-normal with a 16° mean (Hill 1968, VERIFY). The shape of the distribution is assumed; Hill's mean is the only constraint. | 1 | Fit the shape to Hill's histogram once the paper is checked. `exponential` is available as an alternative. |
| A5 | Turn directions are steered toward a goal by a von Mises distribution on the turn's direction around the channel (concentration κ = 2). κ is a tuning parameter, not a physical value. | 1 | Tune κ against published channel statistics (horizontal extent, tortuosity ratio). |
| A6 | The cloud end of the channel sits 0–1.5 km sideways of the strike point (VERIFY). Without this offset, channels are unrealistically plumb vertical. | 1 | Replace with a published distribution of channel inclination. |
| A7 | Each branch is steered toward its own departure direction (κ = 2) and never comes within one segment length of the ground. Branch angle U(20°, 60°), median length 300 m (log-normal, σ = 0.75), length halving at each level of sub-branching, and 0.01 branches per 10 m step are all unsourced placeholders (VERIFY). | 1 | Calibrate against photographic branch statistics. |
| A8 | Energy per unit length is 1×10⁶ J/m on the main channel and in-cloud section; each branch level multiplies it by 0.2 (VERIFY). It is constant along each path. | 1, 2 | Few's model puts the spectral peak near 120 Hz at this energy. Energy decaying with height or branch length could be added. |
| A9 | The in-cloud section is a single near-horizontal walk at the height where the channel starts. It holds its initial heading (κ = 2), a restoring pull keeps it near that altitude (45° tilt per 1 km of error), and it has no branches of its own. It refires on every stroke, like the main channel. | 1, 2 | Branched in-cloud networks are a stretch goal. Check whether in-cloud sections should refire on later strokes. |
| A10 | Later strokes reuse the exact geometry of the main channel (and in-cloud section). Stroke count and interstroke interval are uniform within the configured ranges. | 1, 2 | Real subsequent strokes sometimes take a new ground path. |
| A11 | Each piece of channel radiates a unit-peak N-wave. Its duration T is set so the spectral peak equals Few's f_peak = 0.63 c/R0, using pressure and sound speed at the source height: T = x*/(π f_peak), where x* = 2.0816 is the maximum of j₁. | 2 | Few's constants are VERIFY. A real pulse shape (rounded, asymmetric) would change the spectrum above the peak. |
| A12 | Amplitude is set by energy: a long straight line radiates ηE_ℓ per meter, giving q = sqrt(ηE_ℓρ₀/π)/T. The acoustic efficiency η = 0.002 is VERIFY. It sets the absolute level only, about 50 Pa peak at 3 km. | 2 | Calibrate η against measured thunder levels. Nonlinear losses near the source are ignored (see A13). |
| A13 | Propagation is linear beyond the source: no weak-shock steepening or nonlinear attenuation. | 2 | Stretch goal in the spec. |
| A14 | Sub-segment tortuosity: inside each 10 m segment, a 2D Brownian bridge (pinned at both ends) with 16° mean turn between 1 m pieces, which extends Hill's statistics down to 1 m (VERIFY). Applied in synthesis only, so channel geometry and ground truth are unchanged. Without it, straight 10 m segments cancel almost perfectly off-broadside, leaving isolated claps with roughly 50 dB of silence between them, unlike real thunder. | 2 | Replace with measured small-scale tortuosity if available. Turning it off (`micro_turn_mean_deg: null`) recovers the ideal coherent-segment model. |
| A15 | Later strokes repeat the same energy per unit length as the first stroke. | 2 | Subsequent strokes typically carry less charge, so a per-stroke energy factor could be added. |
| A16 | In Phase 2 every main-channel pulse has the same duration, so the summed spectrum keeps the N-wave's spectral zeros (266, 457, 645 Hz at 1×10⁶ J/m). Phase 3's pressure falling with height varies the duration by about ±40% along the channel and smears them. | 2, 3 | Expected to disappear with a realistic atmosphere. |
| A17 | Mic hardware presets are typical datasheet values (VERIFY): **measurement** 2 Hz–2 kHz, 20 dB SPL self-noise, 140 dB clip, 24-bit; **audio** 20 Hz high-pass, 15 dB, 130 dB, 24-bit; **phone** 100 Hz high-pass, 32 dB, 120 dB, 16-bit. All band edges are 2nd-order Butterworth, causal (analog-like phase). Per-mic tolerance: sensitivity σ of 0.2 / 1 / 2 dB and corner frequency σ of 2 / 10 / 20% (log-normal). | 4 | Replace with measured responses of the actual hardware. Phone AGC and noise suppression are not modeled. |
| A18 | A low-pass edge at or above 0.45 fs is skipped: the 8 kHz recording is already band-limited by synthesis decimation, which plays the role of the recorder's anti-alias filter. | 4 | Run at a higher sample rate to model mic high-frequency response. |
| A19 | Self-noise is white, with the datasheet level taken over 20 Hz–20 kHz (A-weighting ignored) and converted to a density, so it doesn't depend on the sample rate. | 4 | Use measured self-noise spectra (often 1/f below about 100 Hz). |
| A20 | Clock model: τ = (1 + drift)·t + offset per mic. Presets (VERIFY): GPS σ = 1 µs; one shared interface clock with common drift σ = 10 ppm; hand-synced σ = 3 ms with independent drift σ = 20 ppm. The ppm stretch of each pulse is neglected. Jitter is first-order (x + εx′). | 4 | Add per-recorder start-time quantization and temperature-dependent drift. |
| A21 | Position errors are Gaussian per axis (horizontal / vertical σ): surveyed 1 / 2 cm, tape 10 / 5 cm, consumer GPS 3 / 5 m (VERIFY). True heights are clamped at the ground. | 4 | — |
| A22 | Flash-time error (VERIFY): photodiode σ = 10 µs, lightning network σ = 1 ms, 30 fps video uniform ±16.7 ms (the reported time is the center of the first frame showing the flash, so it's unbiased). With frame-start reporting the error is up to 33 ms late, so ranges come out up to about 11 m too long (SPEC.md's example). | 4 | — |
| A23 | Background noise is a 3D isotropic diffuse field: coherence sinc(2fd/c), spectrum pink by default (flat below 0.5 Hz so its power is finite). Its level is set by SNR: band power in 10–300 Hz at the acoustic input, over the thunder window, averaged over mics. | 4 | Real ambient noise is often directional (traffic, surf). |
| A24 | Wind noise (VERIFY): RMS = 0.01 × ½ρU², spectrum flat below U/(1 m) and falling as f^(−5/3) above, coherence exp(−fd/U). Wind speed is a sensor setting; Phase 3 should take it from the wind profile at mic height. | 4 | Calibrate against windscreen measurements (e.g. Strasberg). |
| A25 | Rain noise (VERIFY): independent Poisson drop impacts at 1000 per second, each with exponential amplitude, shaped by a 1st-order 500 Hz high-pass, at a set broadband level over the recording band. | 4 | — |
| A26 | Method A is plane-wave TDOA: within one window, all arriving sound is assumed to come from one direction, in the far field. Windows that mix several directions fail the residual gate. Below about 1 km altitude this is common for distant arrays, because a near-vertical channel's lower part sits at almost one range. Method B (M6) handles multiple directions per window. | 5 | — |
| A27 | Range is c·(t_c − t0), with t_c the energy centroid of the reference window. This puts each point about T/2·c ≈ 0.9 m beyond the pulse onset (N-wave energy is centered at T/2). | 5 | Correct this using the dominant-frequency estimate of T. |
| A28 | SNR is defined by average power over the whole thunder window. Claps dominate it, so the rumble between claps (40–60 dB quieter) sits below the noise even at 25 dB SNR, and coverage falls quickly with noise (dev bolts: 83% → 60% → 27% at ∞ / 25 / 15 dB). Accuracy degrades much less (1.4 → 3.2 → 2.9 m median). | 4, 5, 7 | E3 should also report a per-window (local) SNR. |
| A29 | The strike point is extrapolated from the lowest 8 reconstructed points along their principal direction, with the sideways move capped at 0.5 × height (about 27°). When the lowest points belong to a branch, the estimate can be off by over 1 km (rare; it drives the p100). | 5, 6 | Use the channel skeleton to pick the main trunk. |
| A30 | The atmosphere is horizontally stratified: profiles depend only on height, with no horizontal gradients and no turbulence. | 3 | Turbulence is a stretch goal. |
| A31 | Relative humidity is constant with height, so q falls with temperature aloft. Sound speed uses the sonic temperature T(1 + 0.51q) (Kaimal & Gaynor 1991). | 3 | Use a measured humidity profile. |
| A32 | Pressure is hydrostatic for dry air (virtual temperature neglected: under 0.5% error at 20 °C, 50% RH). | 3 | — |
| A33 | Wind is a power law u_ref((z + 0.1 m)/(10.1 m))^(1/7) (VERIFY), which still gives about 0.5 u_ref at the ground rather than zero, with optional linear veer of direction with height. There is no vertical wind. | 3 | A log-law surface layer and a measured upper profile. |
| A34 | Rays: only the direct (monotone in height) ray and its single ground reflection are modeled. Rays refracted back down from above the source, multiple bounces, and diffraction into the shadow zone are not. Points with no direct ray are silent. | 3 | Diffraction into the shadow is a SPEC stretch goal. |
| A35 | Ray-tube amplitude conserves ρc-weighted energy flux (the O(Mach) moving-medium terms of the Blokhintzev invariant are neglected). Caustics are capped at 10× the straight-line 1/R. | 3 | — |
| A36 | Absorption uses ISO 9613-1, averaged over the heights each path spans (straight-path approximation for the length), and is applied zero-phase (absorption's dispersion is neglected). Emitters share filters within 100 m path-length bins. | 3 | — |
| A37 | The ground is rigid (reflection coefficient 1 by default), with no impedance model. | 3 | Delany–Bazley impedance (spec: optional). |
| A38 | Refraction-aware Method A uses the measured horizontal slowness as the ray's conserved s_h and back-traces it through the *assumed* atmosphere. Any error in the assumed profiles (especially wind) maps directly into position error. | 5 | Self-calibration (E7). |
| A39 | Method B steers in horizontal slowness (a plane wave across the array). Wavefront curvature across the aperture is neglected, so B is a far-field method, like A; C models curvature exactly. | 5 | Use Method C, or a near-field SRP over 3D points, for large or distributed arrays. |
| A40 | Method C's observed times come from pair TDOAs, plus the reference arrival time and the reported t0. All mics are assumed to hear the same feature of the same source. It seeds from Method A's one-direction-per-window fits, so it doesn't separate simultaneous sources. | 5 | Seed C from Method B's detections. |
